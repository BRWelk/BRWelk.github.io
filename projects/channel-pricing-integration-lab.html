How the product evolved

It is no longer just a floor-plan and purchasing demonstrator.

The original case study focused on carrying equipment from a drawing through a field survey, quote, purchasing BOM, and accounting documents. The current application has expanded into a broader workspace that starts before design and continues through post-purchase reconciliation.

The operating problem is continuity. Project information, equipment identity, pricing readiness, commercial assumptions, and accounting ownership should not have to be reconstructed every time work moves from business development to the field, estimating, purchasing, or finance.

Current solution

One controlled equipment chain from opportunity to connected documents.

  • Read-only opportunity discovery with a local pursuit list and direct project handoff
  • Durable floor plans with package placement, cropping, revisions, archive/restore, and linked surveys
  • Reusable equipment packages with independent project copies and revision protection
  • CSV and XLSX equipment-schedule import with exact full-SKU matching and preview-before-apply controls
  • Separate accounting-managed catalog and private estimate-only quote catalog with explicit readiness labels
  • Saved quotes and linked projects that preserve source systems, quantities, prices, margins, and lineage
  • Purchasing drafts that survive refreshes and retain the quote, project, and floor-plan source
  • Background read-only pricing verification against only the active managed catalog
  • Fresh-read safeguards before any explicitly approved QuickBooks sandbox purchasing or cost action
  • Connected PO, bill, payment, and reconciliation history while the accounting platform retains native transaction ownership
  • Administrator and Purchasing roles, protected connector credentials, searchable cost history, and global Search & help

Identity before automation

Full SKU and stable accounting IDs control whether an item is allowed into purchasing.

The current architecture treats the accounting system's active managed item list as the runtime authority for purchase-capable equipment. Estimate-only catalog rows remain useful for surveys and budgetary quotes, but they cannot create a purchasing snapshot or purchase order until independently matched to an eligible active accounting item.

Before a sandbox write, the application freshly re-reads the exact accounting item and verifies its stable ID, full SKU, active state, preferred-vendor scope, current synchronization token, and the corresponding upstream pricing result. Missing or ambiguous pricing fails closed.

Pricing operations

Large catalog checks and cost updates run as recoverable background jobs.

Read-only pricing checks operate only on the current managed catalog, use bounded request batches, preserve a pricing watermark, and expose progress rather than holding one browser request open. A daily organization-level check can refresh the comparison, but it does not write a cost change.

Approved sandbox cost synchronization is separately controlled. Items are checkpointed individually, request pacing and rate-limit recovery are enforced, and an interrupted job does not resume a write by itself after restart. The operator must explicitly resume only the remaining items.

Security & operating boundaries

The integration layer is intentionally narrower than the workflows it coordinates.

The application uses customer-isolated SQLite records, Administrator and Purchasing roles, HTTP-only application sessions, customer-bound encryption for connector credentials, and memory-only OAuth tokens in the development environment. Sensitive credentials are never returned to the browser after storage.

Upstream pricing and ERP-provider access remains read-only. Production QuickBooks writes remain disabled. The portfolio demonstrates only the explicitly guarded sandbox workflows represented in the current source.
